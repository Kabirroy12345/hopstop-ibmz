1. PROBLEM STATEMENT & WHY LEGACY AML FAILS Slide 2 / 6

The Golden-Minutes Gap: Stolen Money Moves in Minutes, AML Runs in Hours

What Breaks Today (Post-Settlement Batch AML)

  • Rapid Multi-Hop Layering: Organized cyber-fraud syndicates use mule accounts to split and forward stolen funds across 3–5 banks within 2 to 10 minutes using instant rails (IMPS/UPI/ACH/Wire).
  • Hours-Late Detection: Traditional AML transaction monitoring runs in hourly or end-of-day batch jobs—flagging the account long after it has been drained to zero.
  • High False Positives on Static Rules: Simple velocity rules flag legitimate corporate payroll fan-out and merchant settlements, overwhelming fraud analysts.
  • 1930 Helpline Recovery Bottleneck: By the time a victim reports a scam and police issue a freeze notice, funds have scattered across dozens of downstream cash-out accounts.

The HopStop Paradigm (In-Transaction Interdiction)

  • Pre-Settlement Scoring (< 1 ms): Score every outgoing transfer inside the payment authorization window before money leaves the first mule account.
  • Causal Graph-Velocity Features: Track per-account 15m/1h/24h fan-in, fan-out, pass-through ratio (money out vs. money in within minutes), and time-since-last-credit in O(1) memory lookups.
  • 3-Tier Alert-Budget Policy: Instead of blanket account freezes that hurt retail users, HopStop issues ALLOW, STEP-UP (OTP / cooling period), or HOLD calibrated to the bank's exact 1% analyst alert capacity.
  • Funds Preserved at Hop 1/2: Stolen savings stay frozen in the first or second hop account so banks and cybercrime cells can recover them intact.

2. SOLUTION ARCHITECTURE & IMPLEMENTATION PIPELINE Slide 3 / 6

End-to-End Co-Located Architecture on IBM Z / LinuxONE (s390x)

1. Payment Stream IBM AMLworld transactions replayed in strict timestamp order. Salted SHA-256 hashing of account IDs at ingestion so no raw PII enters logs or models.
→
2. O(1) Feature Engine In-memory sliding deques (15m, 1h, 24h) compute 12 causal features: fan-in/out, pass-through ratio, time-since-credit, rapid drain flag, Welford Z-score.
→
3. Snap ML / Tensor-Tree 36-tree GBDT scorer using tree_format=auto: targets Telum Integrated Accelerator for AI (libzdnn NNPA) or branch-minimized CPU tensor-trees.
→
4. Policy & Audit Layer Calibrated to 1.0% alert budget: returns ALLOW, STEP_UP, or HOLD + Top-3 Reason Codes + SHA-256 hash-chained audit record.
→
5. Carbon Analyst UI Live queue of held transfers, Multi-Hop Chain Interdiction Graph, and real-time latency telemetry (http://localhost:8080).

Strict Prevention of Label Leakage & Train/Serve Skew

3. HOW HOPSTOP LEVERAGES IBM Z & LINUXONE TECHNOLOGIES Slide 4 / 6

Why IBM Z / LinuxONE Is Core to the Architecture (Not Decoration)

1. Co-Located In-Transaction AI

Core banking and payment switches run on IBM Z. Calling an off-platform cloud GPU/CPU cluster adds 5–30 ms of network serialization, TLS handshakes, and tail-latency spikes—breaking payment SLAs.

Running HopStop directly on the same IBM Z / LinuxONE (`s390x`) platform keeps transaction state in local memory and eliminates the external network hop.

2. Telum NNPA & IBM Snap ML

IBM Telum (z16) and Telum II (z17) integrate an on-chip Integrated Accelerator for AI (NNPA, Facility 165).

HopStop's runtime (hopstop/ibmz_runtime.py) probes libzdnn.so via zdnn_is_nnpa_installed() and configures IBM Snap ML with tree_format="auto", compiling decision trees into flat tensor operations for hardware acceleration with automatic fallback to CPU tensor-trees.

3. Pervasive Security & Audit

Zero Off-Platform Exposure: Sensitive payment graphs never leave the LinuxONE instance.

CPACF Hardware Crypto: Account identifiers are hashed at ingestion with salted SHA-256, and every HOLD / STEP_UP decision is linked into a tamper-evident SHA-256 hash chain ( leveraging CPACF on s390x). Containerized in Dockerfile.s390x for IBM Secure Execution guests.

Hardware Detection & Graceful Portability Across LinuxONE VMs

Because community cloud guest VMs may or may not expose the physical NNPA facility, HopStop automatically inspects /proc/cpuinfo (Facilities 165 & 158) and libzdnn.so at startup, reporting hardware status in the live telemetry console while guaranteeing sub-millisecond inference in both accelerator and CPU tensor-tree modes.
